The problem

Waste invoices are paid on trust — because checking them takes days.

Every charge depends on a ticket, a weight and a contract rate, spread across providers, sites and formats. Without the evidence side by side, overcharges and missed collections go unnoticed.

What it does

Five checks, one ledger, every site.

Waste paperwork turns up as driver tickets, weighbridge slips, provider portals and monthly invoices. WasteLedger reads them, links them, and does the checking your team doesn't have time for.

01

Record extraction

Reads tickets, weight slips, invoices and disposal notes. Low-confidence fields go to a person before they count.

02

Invoice reconciliation

Matches each invoice line to collection tickets, weight records and the contracted rate.

03

Anomaly detection

Flags duplicate entries, missing collections, unusual weights and charges that don't match the contract.

04

Waste trend analysis

Recorded volumes by site, month, stream and provider. Measured and estimated weights are always kept apart.

05

Operations reporting

Exception reports and activity summaries that cite the record behind every number, ready to send to a provider.

Evidence first

If we can't show where a number came from, we don't report it.

Every weight in WasteLedger is labelled with how it was captured. Totals never blend measured and estimated figures into one unlabelled number.

Verified

Weighbridge record with source document

A weighbridge ticket we hold a copy of, linked to a collection ticket.

Supports disposal charges. Counts in measured totals.

Reported

Provider-supplied measurement

For example, an on-board vehicle weight from the contractor's system. We have not checked it independently.

Supports charges, labelled as provider-reported.

Estimated

Calculated from volume

Container size × an assumed density. The assumption and its source are always shown next to the figure.

Never supports a charge. Never mixed into measured totals.

What we don't calculate

WasteLedger does not produce recycling rates, diversion rates, savings or emissions figures from incomplete data. Where a provider supplies them, they are shown as the provider's own statement, with the document they came from.

Platform

Built on AWS, designed for audit.

Source documents are kept unchanged. Extraction, classification and reconciliation each leave a record you can inspect.

Amazon S3

Document vault

Tickets, invoices, weight slips and contracts. Versioned and encrypted, with object lock for the audit trail.

Amazon Textract

Extraction

Expense analysis for invoices, forms and queries for tickets and weight slips. Confidence kept per field.

Amazon Bedrock

Classification & explanation

Sorts documents by type and maps fields. Writes plain-language explanations from the reconciliation results only.

PostgreSQL

Operational ledger

Sites, providers, contracts, tickets, weights, invoices and exceptions, each linked to its source document.

AWS Lambda

Scheduled reconciliation

Runs nightly and when new invoices arrive. Raises exceptions and sends report summaries.

The model never does the maths

Totals, variances and matches come from deterministic code. Bedrock only classifies documents and explains results it is given, and it must cite record IDs.

A person checks uncertain fields

Any field below the confidence threshold waits for review before it can support or dispute a charge.

FAQ

Does WasteLedger pay or dispute invoices automatically?

No. It checks every charge against its ticket, weight and contract rate and flags what doesn’t match. Your team decides what to pay and what to raise.

What problem does it actually solve?

Waste invoices depend on evidence spread across providers and sites, so they’re usually paid on trust. WasteLedger puts each charge next to its evidence and produces exception reports that cite their sources.

Which documents can it read?

Collection tickets, weighbridge records, contracts and invoices in common formats. The pilot confirms formats with your providers.

How is AI used?

Only to read documents into structured records. The reconciliation is deterministic, and every exception lists its sources.

Can it help with sustainability reporting?

Yes — tonnage and diversion figures come from the same verified records used for reconciliation.
